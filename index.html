I am a student of Electrical and Electronic Engineering at the Chittagong University of Engineering and Technology (CUET), Bangladesh. I am drawn to the place where electronics meets software, where a few lines of code can make a circuit sense, decide and respond.

ESP-32 embedded projects

Hands-on builds, each one tested on the breadboard and recorded on video.

PROJECT 01 · MEASUREMENT

Distance Measurement with ESP-32

An ultrasonic sensor sends a pulse and times its echo. The ESP-32 converts the time of flight into centimetres and shows the reading live on an OLED display. I checked the output against a ruler to see how accurate it really is.

How it works
  1. The ESP-32 triggers the HC-SR04 with a 10 µs pulse.
  2. It measures the width of the echo pulse.
  3. Distance = (time × speed of sound) ÷ 2.
  4. The result is drawn on the I²C OLED display.
ESP-32HC-SR04OLED (I²C)Arduino C++

PROJECT 02 · SENSING

Obstruction Detection with IR Sensor

An infrared obstacle sensor watches the path. While it is clear, a green LED stays on. When a hand or object comes close, the ESP-32 switches to a red warning LED and prints “Obstacle Detected” on the serial monitor.

How it works
  1. The IR transmitter emits light and the receiver watches for reflection.
  2. A digital output from the module goes LOW when something is near.
  3. The ESP-32 reads the pin and switches the LEDs accordingly.
  4. The status is also logged over serial for debugging.
ESP-32IR sensorLEDsSerial monitor

PROJECT 03 · INSTRUMENTATION

Tachometer with ESP-32

A contactless RPM meter. The ESP-32 counts pulses from an optical sensor as a motor spins and displays the speed on an OLED screen. I compared it side by side with a commercial handheld tachometer to validate the readings.

How it works
  1. A sensor produces one pulse per rotation (or per blade).
  2. An interrupt on the ESP-32 timestamps every pulse.
  3. RPM = 60 ÷ period between pulses.
  4. The value is smoothed and shown on the OLED.
ESP-32InterruptsOLEDCalibration

PROJECT 04 · CONTROL

Traffic Light Controller

A three-colour traffic signal driven by the ESP-32. It cycles through green, yellow and red with timed delays, which is a clean first exercise in GPIO outputs and sequential state control.

How it works
  1. Three GPIO pins are set as outputs, one per LED.
  2. Green → yellow → red, each held for a fixed time.
  3. The loop repeats forever like a real signal.
  4. Next step: pedestrian button and timing by interrupt.

IDEA LAB · BIG QUESTIONS I ENJOY

Ideas that I keep returning to

Four directions of curiosity: the very small, the very large, the world today and the world of yesterday. Choose a tab to explore.

Wave–particle duality

Light and electrons behave like waves and like particles. In the double-slit experiment, single electrons fired one at a time still build up an interference pattern, as if each one passed through both slits.

“The double-slit experiment has in it the heart of quantum mechanics. In reality, it contains the only mystery.”Richard Feynman, Feynman Lectures, Vol. III

Heisenberg's uncertainty principle

Position and momentum cannot both be known exactly: Δx·Δp ≥ ℏ/2. This is not a flaw of our instruments. It is built into nature, and it limits how small and how fast our electronics can ever be.

“What we observe is not nature itself, but nature exposed to our method of questioning.”Werner Heisenberg, Physics and Philosophy

Superposition and Schrödinger's cat

Until it is measured, a quantum system can exist in a combination of states. Schrödinger's 1935 cat, both alive and dead in the thought experiment, was meant to show how strange this sounds at everyday scales. Qubits in quantum computers use exactly this idea.

“Anyone who is not shocked by quantum theory has not understood it.”Niels Bohr (attributed)

Entanglement

Two particles can share one quantum state so that measuring one instantly fixes what you will find in the other, however far apart they are. Bell's theorem and experiments (Aspect, Clauser and Zeilinger, Nobel Prize 2022) show that no local hidden-variable theory can explain it.

“Spooky action at a distance.”Albert Einstein, letter to Max Born, 1947

Quantum tunnelling

A particle can cross an energy barrier that classical physics says it cannot. It powers the Sun's fusion, tunnel diodes and the flash memory in every phone, and it is also why transistors leak current as they shrink. This is the EEE link I find most beautiful.

“I think I can safely say that nobody understands quantum mechanics.”Richard Feynman, The Character of Physical Law, 1965

Many-worlds interpretation

Hugh Everett (1957) proposed that the wave function never collapses. Every possible outcome happens, and the universe branches. It is one of several rival interpretations, along with Copenhagen and pilot-wave theory, and physicists still debate which is right.

“God does not play dice with the universe.”Albert Einstein, 1926 (paraphrase of his letter to Born)

Black holes

A region where gravity is so strong that, inside the event horizon rs = 2GM/c², even light cannot escape. In 1974 Stephen Hawking showed that black holes should radiate slowly, with temperature T = ℏc³/(8πGMkB), so they can evaporate. What happens to the information that falls in is still unresolved (the information paradox). The first image of a black hole shadow (M87*) came in 2019. See my simulation above.

“Black holes ain't as black as they are painted.”Stephen Hawking

Wormholes

In 1935 Einstein and Rosen found that the equations of general relativity allow a “bridge” joining two regions of spacetime. Real traversable wormholes would need exotic matter with negative energy (Morris and Thorne, 1988), and none has been observed. A modern idea, ER = EPR (Maldacena and Susskind, 2013), suggests that entanglement and wormholes may be two descriptions of the same thing.

“The cosmos is within us. We are made of star-stuff.”Carl Sagan, Cosmos

Extra dimensions

In 1921 Kaluza showed that adding a fifth dimension to Einstein's gravity can also produce electromagnetism, and Klein suggested the extra dimension is curled up too small to see. String theory needs 10 dimensions, and M-theory 11. So far there is no experimental evidence, but the idea keeps unifying gravity with quantum physics on paper.

“Spacetime tells matter how to move; matter tells spacetime how to curve.”John Archibald Wheeler

Time

Time is not universal. Moving clocks run slow, and clocks deeper in gravity run slow too. GPS satellites must correct for a net 38 microseconds per day, or positions would drift by about 10 km daily. Near a black hole horizon, time for a distant observer almost stops. Why time has an arrow at all is tied to entropy and the low-entropy Big Bang.

“The distinction between past, present and future is only a stubbornly persistent illusion.”Albert Einstein, 1955, letter on the death of Michele Besso

Spacetime as one fabric

Minkowski showed in 1908 that space and time merge into a four-dimensional spacetime. Gravity is the curvature of that fabric, and the 2015 detection of gravitational waves by LIGO proved that the fabric can ripple.

“Henceforth space by itself, and time by itself, are doomed to fade away into mere shadows.”Hermann Minkowski, 1908

Keep looking up

Every element heavier than hydrogen and helium was forged in stars and supernovae, including the iron in our blood. Studying the sky is a way of studying our own origin.

“Remember to look up at the stars and not down at your feet.”Stephen Hawking (attributed)

Turning points of the past

1914: The July Crisis and World War I

On 28 June 1914 the assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand in Sarajevo set off a chain of alliances. Within weeks Europe split into the Entente and the Central Powers. About 9–10 million soldiers died. The war ended on 11 November 1918, destroyed four empires (German, Austro-Hungarian, Russian, Ottoman), and the 1919 Treaty of Versailles planted the seeds of the next war. The collapse of the Ottoman Empire also drew many of today's Middle East borders, such as the Sykes–Picot agreement of 1916.

1939–1945: World War II

Germany invaded Poland on 1 September 1939. The war spread across Europe, Africa and Asia. It brought the Holocaust, in which six million Jews were murdered, the Soviet victory at Stalingrad, D-Day (6 June 1944) and, in August 1945, the atomic bombs on Hiroshima and Nagasaki. An estimated 70–85 million people died. The United Nations (1945) and a world divided between the US and the USSR emerged.

1947 and 1971: Partition and Bangladesh

British India was partitioned in August 1947 into India and Pakistan, displacing millions. In 1971, after the Language Movement of 1952 and the war of liberation, East Pakistan became Bangladesh on 16 December 1971. It is the geopolitical story that shaped my own homeland.

1945–1991: The Cold War

The US and USSR never fought directly but competed through proxy wars, the arms race and the space race. The 1962 Cuban Missile Crisis brought the world closest to nuclear war. The Berlin Wall fell in November 1989 and the Soviet Union dissolved in December 1991.

“Every gun that is made, every warship launched, every rocket fired signifies, in the final sense, a theft from those who hunger and are not fed.”Dwight D. Eisenhower, 1953

2001: 9/11 and the new century

The attacks of 11 September 2001 led to the wars in Afghanistan and Iraq and to a generation of security politics. They were followed by the 2008 financial crisis, the Arab Spring (2011) and the return of great-power competition.

“Those who cannot remember the past are condemned to repeat it.”George Santayana, 1905

The Roman Empire

From a small city on the Tiber (traditionally founded 753 BC), Rome became a Republic in 509 BC and an empire under Augustus in 27 BC. At its height under Trajan, around 117 AD, it ruled roughly 5 million km² and tens of millions of people around the Mediterranean. Its power rested on disciplined legions, over 80,000 km of paved roads, aqueducts, concrete and written law. The western half fell in 476 AD.

Legacy: Roman law, Latin, the calendar and the idea of a republic.

Byzantium and Constantinople

Emperor Constantine I refounded the Greek city of Byzantium as Constantinople in 330 AD, on the strait between Europe and Asia, which made it the gate of trade and the capital of the eastern Roman (Byzantine) Empire. Justinian (527–565) rebuilt Hagia Sophia in under six years and reconquered Italy and North Africa. The city survived for over a thousand years behind the Theodosian Walls, was sacked by the Fourth Crusade in 1204, and fell on 29 May 1453 to the 21-year-old Sultan Mehmed II, whose giant cannons broke the walls.

Lesson: location and walls matter, but so do unity and technology.

The Ottoman Empire

Founded around 1299 by Osman I in Anatolia, it conquered Constantinople in 1453, Mamluk Syria and Egypt in 1517, and reached its peak under Suleiman the Magnificent (1520–1566), ruling the Balkans, the Middle East and North Africa and controlling key trade routes. Its army used janissaries and gunpowder artillery, and its millet system let Muslims, Christians and Jews live under their own religious laws. After the failed Siege of Vienna in 1683 it slowly declined, and ended after World War I, with the sultanate abolished in 1922 and the Republic of Turkey founded in 1923.

The British Empire

Beginning with trading companies such as the East India Company (1600) and winning at Plassey in 1757, Britain built the largest empire in history. By around 1920 it covered about a quarter of the world's land and people, thanks to naval power, finance and the Industrial Revolution. It spread railways, English and parliamentary institutions, but also caused famines, forced labour and the slave trade, and the partition of India in 1947. Most colonies became independent between 1947 and 1980.

Of special note to me: Bengal, my own region, was the first large territory the Company ruled.

Ancient Egypt and the Pharaohs

Egypt was united around 3100 BC and was ruled by pharaohs for almost 3,000 years, who were seen as living gods and kept order along the Nile's yearly flood. The Great Pyramid of Khufu (c. 2560 BC, originally 146 m tall) was the tallest building on Earth for nearly 4,000 years. In the New Kingdom, Thutmose III built an empire reaching Syria, and Ramesses II (reigned 66 years) fought the Hittites at Kadesh (c. 1274 BC). Hieroglyphs were deciphered by Champollion in 1822 using the Rosetta Stone, and Cleopatra VII, the last pharaoh, died in 30 BC.

Tutankhamun's nearly intact tomb was found by Howard Carter in 1922.

The Arab world

Twenty-two countries, from Morocco to Oman, share the Arabic language and form the Arab League (founded 1945), with about 450 million people. After Islam spread in the 7th century, Baghdad’s House of Wisdom (8th–13th centuries) became a world centre of science. Al-Khwarizmi gave us “algebra” and “algorithm”, Ibn al-Haytham founded experimental optics, and Fatima al-Fihri founded the University of al-Qarawiyyin in Fez in 859. Today the region matters for its oil and gas, the Suez Canal and the Strait of Hormuz.

Prophet Muhammad ﷺ and the rise of Islam

Muhammad ﷺ was born in Mecca around 570 CE and was known as al-Amin, the trustworthy, before his mission. The first revelation came in the Cave of Hira in 610. After years of persecution he migrated (the Hijra) to Medina in 622, which marks Year 1 of the Islamic calendar. There he built a community with the Constitution of Medina. In 630 Mecca was taken almost without bloodshed and he granted a general amnesty. At his Farewell Sermon in 632 he taught the equality of all people, and he passed away that year in Medina. Within a century, Islam reached from Spain to the Indus.

“We have not sent you except as a mercy to the worlds.”Qur’an 21:107
“An Arab has no superiority over a non-Arab, nor a non-Arab over an Arab… except by piety and good action.”From the Farewell Sermon of the Prophet ﷺ (Musnad Ahmad)

Khalid ibn al-Walid, the Sword of God

A brilliant commander from Mecca’s Quraysh tribe. He led the Quraysh cavalry to victory at Uhud (625), then accepted Islam around 629. At Mu’ta the Prophet ﷺ gave him the title Sayfullah, the Drawn Sword of Allah. As a general under Caliph Abu Bakr he crushed the Ridda rebellion at Yamama (632), conquered much of Iraq in 633, and made a famous forced march across the Syrian desert to reach Damascus in 634. He commanded at Yarmouk (636), where a smaller Muslim army defeated the Byzantines. He is traditionally remembered as never losing a battle, and died in Homs in 642.

Lesson: speed, surprise and discipline can beat numbers.

Napoleon Bonaparte

Born in Ajaccio, Corsica, in 1769, he rose through the army of the French Revolution, seized power in 1799 and crowned himself Emperor in 1804. His Napoleonic Code (1804) shaped legal systems across the world. He beat Austria and Russia at Austerlitz (1805) and dominated Europe, but the 1812 invasion of Russia destroyed his Grande Armée of about 600,000 men. After Waterloo on 18 June 1815 he was exiled to St Helena, where he died in 1821. His Egyptian campaign (1798) also led to the Rosetta Stone.

“Impossible is a word to be found only in the dictionary of fools.”Napoleon Bonaparte (attributed)

Alexander the Great

Born in Pella in 356 BC and tutored by Aristotle, Alexander became king of Macedon at 20. In 334 BC he crossed into Asia and, within 13 years, defeated the Persian Empire at Granicus, Issus and Gaugamela, founded Alexandria in Egypt (331 BC) and marched to the Indus, where his exhausted army refused to go further after the Battle of the Hydaspes (326 BC). He died in Babylon in 323 BC at only 32. His generals split the empire, but the Hellenistic world of Greek language, science and cities that he began lasted for centuries.

“There is nothing impossible to him who will try.”Attributed to Alexander, as reported by later writers

MY STORY

A STORY OF A TEA-SHOP BOY

Resistance, and the voltage to overcome it

My father did not get the success he worked so hard for, yet his greatest success is me. I am the next chapter of his legacy.

01

Roots

My name is Abdullah Mukter (Jewel), and I am currently studying in the Department of Electrical and Electronic Engineering (EEE) at CUET.

I was born and raised in South East Mekhal, a village in Hathazari, Chattogram. My father is an educated man who studied through great hardship. He is very good in mathematics, and in those days he supported his family by tutoring; later he opened a tea shop in our village, where, as a boy, I was a helping hand. My mother is a homemaker and a woman of firm principles.

02

The madrasa

My learning began at a madrasa, the Ishaatus Sunnah Tahfizul Quran Noorani Madrasa. I studied there from the nursery class to class three, and I finished my madrasa life by securing second place in class three.

03

Primary school

Next I joined the primary school of my area, South-East Mekhal Government Primary School, in class four. My roll number was 41 out of 45 students. In the first term exam I stood first, and after standing first again in the annual exam I moved up to class five. I kept first position in every exam and finished primary school with distinction.

In our time there was the PSC examination. I received an A+, the only student in our primary school to do so, and I also passed all three scholarship examinations that I sat for.

04

High school

My journey continued at the high school of my area. I was admitted to class six at South Mekhal High School. There was an admission test in our time, and I began by securing second place among 126 students. The first place went to a girl who was enormously talented!

From class six to class ten I held first position continuously and finished my school life that way. In class eight I passed the JSC examination with a GPA of 4.93. In class nine I took the Science group, but in school I could not do very well in science, because I understood the concepts less. My school life ended with a GPA of 4.72 in the SSC. The result was very disappointing, as I had been expecting A+. I was heartbroken, and I began my college life at Hathazari College.

05

The storm

I forgot to mention something. During my SSC Islamic studies exam, my mother was diagnosed with blood cancer (stage 4 lymphoma). At that time my whole family, and my own state of mind, were completely broken. The income from my father's tea shop was nowhere near enough for her treatment, and my family was financially devastated.

06

College

In our time about 305 or 306 students were admitted to the college, and 121 of them were A+ students, which made me feel very small. Even so, I kept studying with a firm mind, and in the first-year final exam I secured third place. That achievement was enough to turn my life around. I got a completely different idea of my own potential, and a mindset of “I can do it” began to take root.

Because we were not financially comfortable, coaching was a real problem for me in college. At that time Shanta Apu extended a helping hand and taught me Chemistry and Biology for two full years, free of charge. I am most grateful to her.

Going on like this, I became even more focused and kept working tirelessly. In the second-year final exam I stood first, and that achievement raised my confidence greatly.

07

HSC and the admission season

I completed my college life in HSC 2022 with 1206 out of 1300, the highest marks in the college, and a Golden A+. Then the hard phase of admission tests began, and I successfully cracked CUET, Dhaka University and SUST.

From our college, I alone was selected to sit the BUET exam (I had 96% marks in Physics, Chemistry and Math, and BUET wanted 92%). I passed the BUET preliminary and was selected for the written round, but when I did not find my name in the written result, I was somewhat broken. Even so, Allah has given me many things that were beyond my expectations, Alhamdulillah.

08

CUET

And so my journey began with admission to EEE at CUET in October 2023. Since then I have been supporting my family by tutoring almost ten students at the SSC (Science) and HSC (Science) levels, and Alhamdulillah, Allah has kept me well and has carried me along until now.

আমি আব্দুল্লাহ মুক্তার(জুয়েল), বর্তমানে পড়াশুনা করছি EEE Department এ, CUET।আমার বাবা আমার গ্রামে চায়ের দোকান করেন এবং শিক্ষিত একজন ব্যক্তি ছিলো যিনি জীবনে অনেক কষ্ট করে নিজের পড়াশুনা করছে এবং ওই সময়ে নিজে টিউশন করে তার পরিবার কে চালিয়েছে(He was expert in mathematics at that time)। আমার আম্মু একজন গৃহিণী এবং নীতিবান একজন মেয়ে। আমার পড়াশুনার যাত্রা শুরু হয় মাদ্রাসার মাধ্যমে, নাম তার এশায়াতুস সুন্নাহ্ তাহফিজুল কুরআন নূরানী মাদ্রাসা। এইখানে আমি শিশু শ্রেণি থেকে তৃতীয় শ্রেণি পর্যন্ত পড়াশোনা শেষ করি, তৃতীয় শ্রেণিতে দ্বিতীয় স্থান অর্জন করে আমি মাদ্রাসা জীবন শেষ করি।

এরপর আমার পড়াশুনার যাত্রা শুরু হয় আমার এলাকার প্রাইমারী স্কুলের মাধ্যমে, দক্ষিণ পূর্ব মেখল সরকারি প্রাথমিক বিদ্যালয়ে আমি চতুর্থ শ্রেণিতে ভর্তি হয়। রোল ছিল ৪১ মোট ৪৫ জন স্টুডেন্ট এর মধ্যে। প্রথম সাময়িক পরীক্ষায় আমি প্রথম স্থান অর্জন করি, এইভাবে বার্ষিক পরীক্ষায় প্রথম স্থান অর্জন করে আমি পঞ্চম শ্রেণি তে উঠি। এবং কৃতিত্বের সাথে প্রতিটি পরীক্ষায় 1st position ধরে রেখে আমার প্রাথমিক বিদ্যালয়ের জীবনের সমাপ্তি ঘটায়। আমাদের সময় PSC পরীক্ষা ছিলো, আমি A+ পাই একমাত্র আমাদের প্রাথমিক বিদ্যালয়ে,এবং এর পাশাপাশি ৩ টা বৃত্তি পরীক্ষায় ৩ টা তেই উত্তীর্ণ হয়।

এরপরে আমার শিক্ষা জীবনের যাত্রা শুরু হয় আমার এলাকার উচ্চ বিদ্যালয়ে। দক্ষিণ মেখল উচ্চ বিদ্যালয়ে ষষ্ঠ শ্রেণিতে ভর্তি হয়। আমাদের সময়ে ভর্তি পরীক্ষা ছিলো, আমি মোট ১২৬ জন স্টুডেন্ট এর মধ্যে ২য় স্থান অর্জন করে যাত্রা শুরু করি। আমাদের মধ্যে প্রথম ছিল এক মেয়ে, প্রচুর ট্যালেন্টেড ছিলো! যাই হোক,আমি ক্লাস ৬ থেকে ক্লাস ১০ পর্যন্ত লাগাতার 1st position ধরে রেখেই আমার স্কুল জীবন শেষ করি। ক্লাস ৮ এ JSC পরীক্ষায় আমি জিপিএ 4.93 পেয়ে উত্তীর্ণ হয়। ক্লাস 9 এ আমি সায়েন্স নি। কিন্তু স্কুল জীবনে আমি বিজ্ঞান নিয়ে খুব একটা ভালো করতে পারিনি,কনসেপ্ট কম বুঝতাম। যাই হোক এসএসসি তে 4.72 পেয়ে আমার স্কুল জীবন শেষ হয়। এবং আমার রেজাল্টটা ছিল খুবই হতাশাজনক, আমি A+ আশা করছিলাম। অনেক ভেঙে পরি,এবং নিজের জীবনের কলেজ জীবনের যাত্রা শুরু করি হাটহাজারী কলেজ দিয়ে। আমাদের সময়ে মোট ৩০৫/৩০৬ জন শিক্ষার্থী ভর্তি হয় এবং তার মধ্যে ১২১ জন A+ প্রাপ্ত স্টুডেন্ট ছিলো যেখানে নিজেকে অনেক ছোট মনে হচ্ছিলো। তারমধ্যে নিজের পড়াশুনা খুব শক্ত মনে চালিয়ে যেতে থাকি এবং 1st year final পরীক্ষায় আমি তৃতীয় স্থান অর্জন করি। এই অর্জন টা আমার জীবনের মোড় ঘুরিয়ে দেওয়ার জন্য যথেষ্ট ছিলো,নিজের potential সম্পর্কে সম্পূর্ণ ভিন্ন ধারণা পেয়েছিলাম। I can do it, এই ধরনের মনোভাব চলে আসতে থাকলো।

এইখানে একটা কথা বলতে ভুলে গেছিলাম , এসএসসি এর ইসলাম ধর্ম পরীক্ষার সময় আমার আম্মুর ব্লাড ক্যান্সার (4th stage, lymphoma)ধরা পরে,ওই সময়ে আমার পুরো পরিবার,আমার মন মানসিকতা সম্পূর্ণ রূপে ভেঙে যাই। আমার আব্বুর চায়ের দোকান থেকে উপার্জিত অর্থ উনার চিকিৎসার জন্য মোটেও যথেষ্ট ছিলো না,এই সময়ে আমার পরিবার আর্থিকভাবে পুরোপুরি ভেঙে যাই।

কলেজ জীবনে আর্থিকভাবে সচ্ছল না থাকায় কোচিং করা নিয়ে ভালোই সমস্যা হচ্ছিলো তার মধ্যে আমাকে সাহায্যের হাত বাড়িয়ে দেন Shanta Apu, পুরো ২ বছর আমাকে Chemistry এবং biology পড়িয়েছিলেন ফ্রী তে। উনার প্রতি আমি সবচেয়ে বেশি কৃতজ্ঞ। এইভাবে চলতে চলতে আমার পড়াশুনায় আমি আরও মনোযোগ হয়,এবং নিরলস পরিশ্রম করে যাচ্ছি নিজের পড়াশুনা নিয়ে। 2nd year final পরীক্ষায় আমি প্রথম হয়। এই অর্জন আমার confidence অনেক বের যাই এবং এইভাবে নিজের HSC (২০২২) তে আমি ১৩০০ তে ১২০৬(highest number in college) পেয়ে Golden A+ সহকারে আমার কলেজ জীবন যাত্রা শেষ করি।

এরপর আমার জীবনের কঠিন যাত্রা শুরু হয় ভর্তি পরীক্ষার phase নিয়ে এবং আমি কৃতিত্বের সাথে CUET,Dhaka University, SUST crack করি। আমাদের কলেজ থেকে একমাত্র আমি BUET এর পরীক্ষায় বসার জন্য সিলেক্টেড হয়( I have 96% marks in physics, chemistry, Math and BUET want 92%)। BUET এর প্রিলি পাস করে রিটেন এর জন্য সিলেক্ট হয়,তবে রিটেন ফলাফলে নিজের নাম না পেয়ে কিছুটা ভেঙে পরি। তারপরেও আল্লাহ্ আমাকে অনেক কিছুই দিয়েছেন যা আমার এক্সপেক্টেশন ও ছিলো না, Alhamdulillah।

এইভাবে আমার যাত্রা শুরু হয় CUET এর EEE তে ভর্তির মাধ্যমে ২০২৩ সালে(অক্টোবর মাসে)। এরপর থেকে টিউশন করে আমি আমার পরিবার কে চালাচ্ছি এবং Alhamdulillah আল্লাহ্ যথেষ্ট ভালো রেখেছেন,আমাকে চালিয়ে নিচ্ছেন এখনো পর্যন্ত।

— আব্দুল্লাহ মুক্তার (জুয়েল)
